In the Hetmanate Federation, one can obtain the status of a slave by violating the Federation's criminal code. In case of a crime, a judge, with the assistance of artificial intelligence, assigns a term of labor days, during which the person is transferred to slave status.

# Path to Slavery

Any commoner (non-Cossack) can become enslaved for violating the criminal code of the Hetmanate Federation. If guilt is proven, the convicted person receives a certain number of labor days, which they are obligated to work off on assigned tasks according to distribution. The heavier the crime, the more labor days the offender receives and the more grueling the work they may be assigned to. For example, for theft of another's property, a criminal receives 300 labor days on light work (loader, cleaner, harvest collector). For murder — 5000 labor days, with assignments including uranium mines, cargo holds of starships containing nuclear waste, and the like.

For light or moderate punishments, if there is a family, they may relocate together with the convicted man or woman and live near them.

# Progress of Slavery

After sentencing, the new slave's private assets are frozen. They are sent to a distribution center where, according to requests from work collectives, palatinate administrations, and other establishments, work orders are formed specifying the tasks and number of labor days required. Slaves are selected at random and sent to work, first being branded on the forehead with a number indicating the article under which they were punished (if multiple, the most severe), along with a QR code containing the slave's profile. When scanned, one can learn how many days remain, whether the slave has fled, and other details.

# Exit from Slavery

After the last labor day ends at 00:00, a slave exits their enslaved status and returns to the social class from which they entered slavery. The former slave is considered rehabilitated, "clean," and the rules of slave management may no longer be applied to them. From that day forward they are free and may engage in any activity. The tattoo fades over time.

# Restrictions of a Slave

- A slave cannot acquire new private property.
- A slave cannot move freely.
- A slave is obligated to perform the work they are assigned according to their sentence.
- A slave cannot dispose of their property until they exit this status.

# Rights of a Slave

- A slave has the right to food
- A slave has the right to a bed
- A slave has the right to medical care
- A slave has the right to clothing

All these rights are provided by the establishments that ordered the labor days from the Federation.

# Slave's Property

If a person sentenced to slavery had private property and has no heirs, it is confiscated for the benefit of the Federation. If there is a family, the right to property passes according to inheritance law. Usually, if a family wishes to follow the head of the family to the place of punishment, they sell it and relocate (if the place of punishment is on another planet).

# Ordering Labor Days

Work collectives, manufacturing enterprises, military contingents, and others may order labor days from slave administrators. Distribution is controlled by representatives of the Sich. Collectives submit requests such as "3000 labor days for logging in the palatinate." The distribution center sends the request to other planets and (rarely) systems. Slaves are found whose sentences allow them to perform these tasks. Collectives pay the Sich for the slave's hiring and receive them. The collective is obligated to provide for the slave's rights, such as access to medical care, food, and shelter.

# Legal Execution

For serious crimes (murder, rape, incitement to rebellion, sabotage), the convicted person receives labor days of the highest category, which allows sending the slave to perform deadly work — labor in cargo holds of starships containing thermonuclear waste, uranium mines, reactor maintenance. Usually, average life expectancy in such work does not exceed one year. Therefore, the slave is practically sentenced to death, but with usefulness.

# Status of Cossacks and Slavery

No Cossack can be transferred to slave status. Instead of punishment by labor days, a convicted Cossack is stripped of ranks, may be [[../Military Affairs/Nullification|Nullified]], or even stricken from the Cossack registry entirely, transferring the convicted person to commoner status. However, for serious crimes, a Cossack may be sentenced to death, physical punishment, loss of service pay, and reduction of land allotment.
