# Encounter in the Steppe

Bohdan Tytar dismounted from his bot-horse and began studying the tracks pressed into the grass. The strap of his respirator had slipped to his ear, and he pulled the mask from his face.

"You can take your respirators off too — we rest here." Through a light cough from the shift in breathing mixture, Bohdan's voice didn't sound particularly confident. "Next breathing cycle in four hours."

His two comrades also dismounted and coughed the moment they pulled the respirators from their faces. The gas composition on Loca Deserta-17[^wild-field] already measured 72% similarity to the air of Old Earth. So there was no need to wear breathing masks continuously. But if a man breathed this planetary atmosphere for more than ten hours straight, the oxygen saturation in his blood would begin to drop precipitously. Within a day or two, a man would first succumb to weakness and vertigo, and then — to a quiet sleep. Not a fatal one, but on a battlefield, sleep is death.

"Our people didn't finish the air-processing here — another year and it would've been like Old Earth." One of the Cossacks, named Opanas Kelep, cleared his throat and also began studying the tracks on the ground.

"That's the truth. Maybe someday they'll finish the air for us too." Bohdan crouched near the edge of a stream and stared at the tracks again. "But as far as I know, the terraforming stations were smashed during the first raid on the colony. I had acquaintances who worked there — they say orbital bombs flattened all the air-generation adits. Every single one."

"Damned narrow-eyes[^narrow-eyes] — don't they need oxygen on this colony themselves, if they want to take it?"

"The devil knows — maybe they've tinkered with their genetic modifications enough that they can breathe ox farts." Dmytro Hordynia, their third man, joined the conversation but still hadn't gotten off his horse, peering out into the steppe.

"Enough jawing. Back to the task at hand." Bohdan Tytar, as the otaman[^otaman] of the plastun[^plastun] squad, politely cut off the chatter that was breaking their concentration.

He walked several meters to the side, following the tracks from a caterpillar tread. Around the impression left in the ground by the heavy machinery they'd been hunting, half a dozen more tracks were visible — most likely from wheels. "Ox-trembling hell, they've got a whole warband here. I thought it was just one melt-carrier[^melt-carrier] chugging through the steppe."

They were clearly left by Empire of the Sun[^empire-sun] equipment that had passed right through a small stream in the middle of a deep gully.

"The narrow-eyes were here about half an hour ago — the tracks have already been slightly washed by the current. Two more hours till sunset — we'll strike at dawn." Bohdan exhaled loudly, stood up, and immediately slipped on a slick stone.

"By the Star Sich[^star-sich], Bohdan — we still need you." His third battle-comrade, Dmytro Hordynia, also dismounted, but before stepping down, checked the ground first.

"I hate this sector. Stony steppe — grass grows in patches somehow. Always damp, and these little pebbles — like anti-personnel mines." Bohdan rubbed his knee, which had started aching after five hours of pursuit of the melt-carrier, and now this cursed stone had to turn up.

The other two haiduks[^haiduk] had already climbed off their horses. The pebbles crackled under their heavy iron boots. Each disconnected the neural-link wire that coupled them to their horse's control module. On the forehead of each bot-horse was a small bronze trident[^trident], and below it — pressed into the metal — the name of the regiment and sotnia[^sotnia]: "Spears. Plastun-12." Not one of the bots even stirred.

Bohdan Tytar was the otaman of a small plastun scout squad, which had now been two days deep in a raid behind the lines of the Empire of the Sun. Its forces had encircled the only hive-city on the colony planet "Loca Deserta-17" in a wide crescent — a classic Cossack pincer, but turned against its own masters. Their squad had been caught at rest when the invasion began, and within three hours of the alarm, the full regiment of "Zbarazh Spears"[^zbarazh] had mustered to arms.

The attacker had broken through orbital defenses in the opening days of the assault, dragging the heavy atmospheric flyer[^flyer] battles into an air war that had not ceased since. For Tytar and his plastuns, this was already their second combat rotation. When the imperials managed to land their mobile spaceport, the colony surface became a killing ground.

Enormous — simply colossal — ships, three to four hundred meters long, were ferrying troops and war matériel from the invasion fleet in orbit down to the surface for the siege of the only hive-city. The Hets[^hets] had erected several belts of layered defense. The most critical was a fortified trench line stretching seventy kilometers between two natural obstacles — the ocean on the left and impassable thickets on the right. Fighting now raged along the entire length of this line. The earthworks would have been familiar to any Cossack who had ever dug in on the Great Meadow of Old Ukraine.

The attackers were reinforcing their siege force daily; it had even come to the deployment of super-heavy titan-tanks on their side. The mission of the plastuns — light, mobile infantry, the eyes and ears of the Hetmanate army — was to interdict the free movement of enemy detachments and saboteur units, and to bleed the enemy's manpower and materiel base day by day, raid by raid. It was the oldest Cossack art: strike from the grass, vanish into the grass.

It was the trail of one such column that the plastuns had stumbled upon two days ago. It was a convoy transporting melt-charges for tanks and rifles. About a day ago, atmospheric flyers had raided it, destroying half the column — but the trucks with enormous barrels of plasma had somehow survived the rocket salvo and cannon fire. A miracle, or a curse deferred.

Dozens, even hundreds, of such convoys dragged their cargo daily from the spaceport to the hive's defense perimeter. The road was far from short, because the imperials quite reasonably understood that approaching closer than five hundred kilometers to the hive meant suicide for their spaceport, which was constantly displacing to evade orbital strikes. A walking fortress, like the Tatar camps that once roamed the Wild Field.

The order for the second raid Bohdan Tytar received personally from the sotnyk[^sotnyk] of the "Zbarazh Spears." The first raid had been catastrophic: their unit had been detected by the imperials' atmospheric reconnaissance, and within just three hours, two ballistic missiles had impacted the haiduk camp. Half of the twenty-man unit had died on the spot; the rest had barely managed to break contact from pursuit and slip back inside the defense line.

This time their unit numbered only three. Bohdan Tytar — otaman; Dmytro Hordynia — operator of the heavy shoulder-launched incinerator "Hornet-42," which hung on the right flank of his bot-horse; and Opanas Kelep — designated marksman and signals operator. The objectives were no longer as sweeping as before.

The mission of the combat patrol was to locate and destroy just one transport asset and, if the tactical situation permitted, inflict enemy casualties. The melt-carrier was an excellent target for their Hornet-42. "We'll bivouac here for the night. Near the stream you can hear machinery movement clearly, and our biologists say this water's drinkable." Bohdan Tytar turned to his men and began pulling kit from the saddlebags on his bot-horse.

Hordynia and Kelep also began rummaging through their bags, retrieving ration packs and sleeping rolls. Then all three lowered their horses to the ground: the bots folded their mechanical iron legs and lay down on their bellies, like camels — or like the war-horses of the old Zaporozhian Host settling in the tall grass before dawn.

Bohdan pulled out a sealed ration pack and settled onto his sleeping mat. "At 04:00 we move out. I estimate within the hour we'll close with the melt-carrier, engage, and break contact. Let's eat now and grab a couple hours of sleep. What did you draw in your ration pack?"

"I got a mixture of crap, drenched in crap." Dmytro replied gloomily.

"And I got fried boar ears, cured balyk fillet, 'KozakBread' pastries, and a 'Prazhsky' torte." Opanas replied with a completely stone-faced expression.

"Oh, if only... if only." Bohdan's mouth watered just at the names.

"It is what it is. Good thing there's anything at all."

"Dmytro, you should've been a poet, not a plastun." Bohdan reached into the pack, pulled out a spoon, and began eating something resembling buckwheat porridge — soldier's kasha, the eternal companion of every campaign.

All three silently choked down their rations. The food was actually nutritious, didn't irritate the stomach, but when you've been eating the same thing dry for two weeks straight, even torte becomes revolting.

"Here's what I'm thinking." Tytar rinsed his mouth from his flask after the porridge and continued. "Opanas, you'll prep the Hornet in advance so we can ride in, acquire the target, sting, and exfiltrate. If there's a large secondary detonation — we'll decide on liquidating enemy personnel based on the tactical picture. We withdraw immediately toward the wall. In case of wounds or losing a horse, we assess on the spot. No spare horses — we'll try two on one... we'll manage somehow. We don't think about capture, but war is war..."

"If my wound is serious, leave me behind — I'll handle it myself. Better someone gets out of here." Dmytro stared at the ground, then twitched and stuffed the emptied pack into his kit.

"None of that." Bohdan cut him off. "Did you forget the punishment for abandoning your own?"

"Yes, I know. It's a shame — in that squad of ten that left two plastuns behind, two of my friends were among them. Both were executed in front of my eyes when they came back." Dmytro opened his pack and shoved the empty flask inside. "And if the colonel issues them an infamy[^infamy] as well, I don't know how their parents will survive it."

"So — so that our own parents don't have to think about that — no one gets left behind. We came together, we leave together. That's the Cossack way." Bohdan finished his mixture, licked the spoon clean, and stowed the trash in his pack. "And another thing: keep your ground-signature minimal. Their scouts don't waste time — we hunt them, they hunt us. Just like it was on the Great Meadow three centuries ago."

"Agreed. We act by the situation." Bohdan spread his sleeping mat next to his horse. "Hordynia — you take first watch. Then Kelep. I'll take the dead-hour shift. Stand down until 03:00."

Bohdan lay down on the mat and fell instantly asleep from exhaustion. Hordynia removed his samopal[^samopal] from the saddle, clipped two power cells to his chest armor plates, and moved behind a large stone, concealing himself there. A plastun on watch — the oldest ritual of the borderlands.

The planet "Loca Deserta-17" had been habitable for about seven years. They'd even started growing their own food here, though not enough to cover all needs. The terrain resembled the homeland steppe — with stone ridges, very ancient mountains scattered here and there, and an enormous ocean that covered almost the entire surface of the planet. A single continent bore the hive-city on its shore; in the north, tall mountains rose, covered with moss and various ferns. From the mountains stretched a plateau, where all of the Hetmanate Federation's mining and extraction operations were concentrated.

The farther south one went, the better the land became. And around the hive itself, grain fields stretched to the horizon — fields that any Cossack from the black-earth belt of Old Ukraine would have recognized with a pang of longing. Now, after the attack, the beautiful yellow fields were slashed with haiduk tracks; here and there, preparations for abandoning the first defense line and withdrawing to the colony's inner perimeter were underway.

The damned Sunflowers[^sunflowers] had set their spaceport five hundred kilometers from the hive. The Hets couldn't hit it with rocket ordnance — the narrow-eyes defended the spaceport well, and it kept changing its firing position. In general, a mobile spaceport, when you control the skies, gives the attacker an enormous tactical advantage. It can only be struck with heavy rockets, but for that you need to know the precise location. And to know it, you need orbital satellites or reconnaissance rockets... which get shot down by atmospheric and space aviation a hundred kilometers before they could reach the probable target area. A problem the Zaporozhian Cossacks would have understood perfectly: a moving Tatar camp was just as hard to catch with artillery.

And the Hets' military engineers were no fools either. They devised a cunning plan: they launched reconnaissance rockets and drones into different sectors of the continent and observed where exactly they were being shot down. This allowed a "dead zone" of seventy-five kilometers in diameter to appear on the tactical map — where no drone had managed to penetrate. Most likely, that's where the walking spaceport was roaming.

Hordynia, after returning from the first sortie, had managed to witness the launch of massive two-hundred-meter rockets that had fired from the adits north of the hive-city. Even from that distance — some thirty to forty kilometers — everyone could hear the building roar of the boost engines deep in their chests. Four rockets had been launched in a single salvo.

At that moment Dmytro had been seized by the fierce hope that the rockets would reach their target and at least partially relieve the pressure on the first defense line, and the haiduks might somehow push the enemy back from their only city... But later that evening, a rumor had rippled through the townspeople and haiduks that only two of the rockets had managed to reach the target area... where the target no longer was.

That night, the enemy had struck the mountain ridge with orbital fire, where the launch adits were located. A few bombs were also dropped on the city itself, but the anti-orbital defenses managed to intercept them before they reached the altitude of atmospheric aviation.

As though someone had heard Dmytro's thoughts — on the black-blue sky, he noticed three stars moving with extraordinary speed across the dark dome of space in a single direction. Stars that moved in formation were not stars at all.

"Either ours. Or not ours." Hordynia muttered quietly to himself, shifting position to ease the numbness in his right leg. His samopal slipped accidentally from his knees and drove its barrel into the grass and soil.

He slowly raised the weapon and began picking out the dirt that had fouled the barrel with his finger. Hordynia himself came from a haiduk lineage of plastuns. From the ancient days of the Hetmanate's expansion of power on Old Earth, all the old Terek Cossacks[^terek] had joined the ranks of the Great Hetman Vyhovskyi, receiving privileges and a seat on the Kish Council[^kish] in return. Since then, every man in his family had served in the plastun scout forces, with the exception of only a few great-grandfathers who, for whatever reason, had not done so. His blood carried the memory of a dozen generations of frontier warriors.

Hordynia jerked sharply when he saw a flash of light on the horizon, and a few seconds later the sound of an explosion reached him.

"What's this now..." the haiduk continued muttering to himself and rose, leaning on the boulder.

He raised the samopal to his shoulder, pressed his eye to the scope, trying to make out what was unfolding on the steppe horizon. Unfortunately, the thermal sight only reached a kilometer, and the ordinary optical lenses in such darkness gave no better result than his own eyes.

A few more explosions lit up the sky from below, and the distant crackle of gunfire reached his ears with a delay of a good ten seconds.

Dmytro got up and walked over to the sleeping Bohdan Tytar. He shook him lightly by the shoulder, and Bohdan snapped his eyes open — completely alert, no grogginess — staring at Hordynia.

"Ox-trembling hell — can't a man sleep. What time is it?"
"Half past eleven."
"Show me where the explosions are coming from."

Tytar rose, pulled a sizeable pair of binoculars from his personal kit, and pressed them to his eyes, peering toward the horizon Dmytro was indicating.

After a minute of observation, the sky lit up again with flashes, and the distant rumble of detonations rolled to the ears of both haiduks.

"I can see ours. Either they've hit some armor, or the armor is driving them across the steppe." The binoculars' tenfold digital zoom allowed him to distinguish only large objects at that range. "Either way, we need to reinforce them and link up. We prepare for contact — and they're heading our way."

Bohdan lowered the binoculars, climbed down from the boulder he'd been standing on at full height, and looked at Hordynia.

"Wake Kelep. His Hornet is going to earn its keep. With that firefight going, our target has most likely already woken up too — so we won't catch anyone by surprise."

Kelep woke the same way as Bohdan: snapping open completely unsleepy eyes, with no sharp movements, sighs, or gasps. Years of training, practically from childhood, had given the plastuns complete somatic control in extreme moments — like "a large mustachioed uncle with a topknot and a samopal shaking you by the shoulder in the middle of the night in the steppe, deep behind enemy lines." The old Cossack conditioning: wake ready, or never wake at all.

"Kelep — up. Another plastun element is in a firefight. We need to reinforce them. Ready the Hornet for immediate action. Enemy armor is involved."

Kelep, silently, as if receiving such an order in the middle of the night were perfectly routine, rose, rolled up his sleeping bag with the mat, and stuffed it behind the horse's saddle. Then he walked around to the right side and opened the sizeable case containing the Hornet launch unit.

It was a meter-long launch tube. Along the middle of the tube were several buttons and a usage manual — so simple that even a concussed rookie djura[^djura] still stunned from his first skirmish, who just yesterday was twisting horses' tails, could handle it. On the open lid, nestled in soft-plastic grooves, lay three rounds for the Hornet. Externally they looked like ordinary iron slugs. Which, essentially, they were.

None of the optical targeting systems found in the "Bumblebees" used by space haiduks were present here. Just a body shell — inside, seven kilograms of high-explosive compound. And another kilogram of plasma dust, which would incinerate everything in the detonation's epicenter down to the molecular level.

"Should I prep it for combat right now? Or will I have time on approach?" Kelep, leaning across the horse, asked Tytar, who was already pulling on his helmet and the respirator with its proper breathing mixture.

"Prep it all now. The firefight is moving straight toward us — we'll hit from horseback." Tytar pulled the respirator down to his beard, revealing his thick fair mustache — the traditional Cossack *vusa* — so Kelep could hear him better. "We switch the vox to ten meters. No unnecessary radio silence breaks. According to intel from signals reconnaissance, the narrow-eyes have learned to track even such weak local voxes through their recon drones."

"Understood." Kelep and Hordynia answered simultaneously.

The gunfire was getting ever closer. The sky no longer lit up as much from explosions, but the plastuns clearly distinguished the fire from haiduk laser rifles and an enemy rapid-firer — most likely the Sunflowers'.

In just four minutes after the first flashes, all three were combat-ready and mounted. Kelep, as the designated Hornet gunner, loaded it with one round and raised the launch tube to his right shoulder. He gripped it firmly by the forward handgrip that protruded from below. The warhead itself stuck out in front of the tube and glinted faintly under the stars of the "Loca Deserta-17" colony. The launcher was recoilless. Kelep was preparing to fire straight from a gallop. Hordynia, as the second crew member, checked once more the box containing three additional charges for the Hornet, then flicked the safety off his laser samopal, which he always kept at the ready.

"They're even closer to us now. The Sunflowers have run into another one of our patrols and are driving them across the steppe. Our people are still holding. Here's the plan: we fly straight at them, but come in from the left flank. I can see two mechanized infantry vehicles — one of them is a tarantula[^tarantula]. The other — unclear, but wheeled. Kelep — first target is the tarantula. It can change direction at any moment and is highly maneuverable. Then you engage the other vehicle, whatever it is. A few dozen meters out, I'll try to reach ours on the vox so we don't shoot each other to pieces. Let's go! For the Stars and the Sich!"

"For the Stars and the Sich!"[^battle-cry]

All three bot-horses gathered speed and raced toward the glow that was growing larger by the moment. The delay between the muzzle flashes and the explosions shrank with each dull strike of their bot-horses' metal hooves against the alien soil.

The final minutes before contact stretched slowly — the old combat dilation, familiar to every soldier since the first Cossack picked up a saber. Approaching the firefight, the plastuns could already make out half a dozen riders performing the finest feats of combat trick-riding — the ancient art of *dzhigitovka*[^dzhigitovka] — dodging the salvos of melt-fire, laser bolts, and grenades that the narrow-eyes were pouring on them from their fighting machines.

On the tarantula, hanging half out of hatches, sat three Sunflowers firing at the Hets. Several spotlights on each vehicle tried to catch the silhouettes of horses in the night steppe. The imperials weren't going for the kill. They were herding, keeping their distance, again and again cutting down those who slowed. The cruelty of hunters who knew they had all the time in the world.

The plastuns returned fire as best they could, but couldn't inflict significant damage or penetrate the armored machines. When a laser or plasma shot from a samopal struck near the silhouette protruding from a vehicle hatch, it would duck down for a few seconds, then reappear. The silhouette would vanish for a few seconds, then pop back up in the hatch — like a target on an old shooting range.

"Plastun-12 — moving to assist. Pull the vehicles left of your position. We'll strike."

Bohdan Tytar opened to a longer vox broadcast, trying to reach the plastuns. One of them had just been cut in half by a melt-machinegun burst — horse and all. The impact with the ground sent the dead man's helmet flying, revealing a long gray oseledets[^oseledets] — the mark of a Cossack warrior, visible even in death.

"GOING LEFT!" — a breathless, unfamiliar voice that broke into a shout answered briefly over the vox.

The surviving plastuns began shifting to the side, drawing the imperial vehicles with them and exposing them to a flanking strike from the Hornet.

"Kelep — at your discretion. You go first, we're second. Work through all three charges. While they haven't spotted us, the first strike will be a surprise."

Opanas Kelep, steering the horse with his left hand and legs, was circling ever deeper under the right flank of the tarantula, which had not yet detected the new threat. The Sunflowers sticking out of the hatches were gleefully firing at the plastuns and calling out to each other about something. The roar of the mechanical legs of the spider-tarantula was so loud that the approach of the three horses went entirely unheard. About a hundred meters from the enemy's machinery, Kelep turned his horse ninety degrees and drove it straight at the target with all his strength. Riding at a gallop with a shoulder-launched weapon was punishing — his legs cramped from the strain, but he had to compensate for the swaying and bouncing of the horse at full speed. Opanas released the reins he'd been holding in his left hand, shifted it to the forward handgrip of the launcher, and pressed his eye to the large collimating sight.

Breath in. Breath in. Breath out. Breath out. Breath in.
Slowing his breathing ever more under the meditative training chant of "Prih-Skok"[^prih-skok], he finally minimized the swaying of the sight and the tremor in his hands and legs. He gently pressed the trigger — and the rocket, carrying plasma dust and high explosive, left the tube with a painful, sharp whistle and flew straight into the mounting mechanism of the tarantula's front leg.

The machine's standard defense systems tried to intercept it: steel balls fired from side ports in the armor and exploded a few meters from the hull, trying to destroy the dangerous projectile with a cloud of fragmentation. All in vain.

From the detonation of the melt-substance, the front leg of the spider was melted and nearly torn away, and the machine — given its enormous mass and momentum — pitched its hull straight into the ground. The impact sent two Sunflowers flying from the hatches, and they soared a dozen meters before slamming into the earth. The third narrow-eye was unable to exit the hull — instead, the impact snapped his spine. His body lay on the armor in an unnatural position, bent at ninety degrees. From the shattered visor of his helmet, a stream of narrow-eyed blood immediately began seeping across the scorched metal.

The two Sunflowers who'd been luckier only had their luck hold for a few seconds. Two bursts from laser samopals — from Tytar and Hordynia — terminated their attempts to rise.

"Kelep, you're a master! Load another one before they recover!" Tytar began laying down suppressive fire on the tarantula, whose pilot was trying to right the machine and bring it up on its remaining five legs.

The Hets had encountered such zoo-machines[^zoo-machines] for the first time on "Loca Deserta-17." Until then, rumors had circulated that the Empire of the Sun was developing strange machines resembling animals and spiders, but to actually knock one out in the field... The tarantula was extraordinarily maneuverable and had immense off-road capability. Even losing two — or even three — limbs, if not all on the same side, didn't turn it into scrap metal. Tytar had personally seen one of the narrow-eyes' tarantulas continue fighting and maneuvering across the field with one leg on the left and two on the right. A mechanical cockroach from hell.

"Plastuns — spread right and left, we need to fix the other vehicle!" Tytar, seeing the chaos in the ranks of the squad that the imperials had been chasing, took tactical command of the engagement over the vox.

"Executing! But don't finish off the tarantula — our squad leader's in there!"

"What? He's captured?" Tytar replied in surprise over the vox.

"Yes! We'll sort it out later!"

"I heard everything — I'll work on the tarantula's legs!" Opanas Kelep, sheltering behind a large boulder — the steppe was littered with them — was waiting for Hordynia to load him another grenade. Doing it alone without dismounting was nearly impossible. And there was no time.

Dmytro rode over at once, slung his samopal over his shoulder, and bent toward the ammunition case on his horse's side.

"Loading a charge!"

"Go — the tarantula's almost back on its feet!" Kelep laid the launch tube on his right shoulder and turned slightly toward Hordynia so he'd have an easier angle to insert the new charge.

Dmytro unclipped two latches on the right case of his bot-horse, pulled out the first round, removed the plastic safety cap from the signal connector, and inserted the melt-grenade into the launch tube.

"Ready! I'll draw their fire — you follow me!" Dmytro Hordynia pulled the laser samopal from his shoulder and opened fire the moment he sprang out from behind the stones.

The response was swift: one of the guns on the tarantula began firing back, but due to the severe pitch of the hull, the gunner couldn't quite crank the sight around, and all the shots landed ten meters wide of the plastuns.

The second rocket from the Hornet struck the spider's rear leg, cleanly tearing it from the hull. The detached chunk of metal from the leg drove straight into the central, last manipulator of the machine, putting it out of action too.

"Now that's marksmanship!" Tytar shouted over the vox. "Plastuns — how are you holding up?"

Through the darkness, the smoke from the explosions, and the boulders piled everywhere, Tytar had lost track of the second vehicle. It had vanished from sight the moment the tarantula went down.

"Two of us left! Another was cut down by their armor's machinegun!"

"Can you see the vehicle? Fire upward — where are you?" Tytar wanted to switch Kelep to a new target as quickly as possible.

"Damn — they've unloaded infantry from the armor! I can see a whole section!"

Tytar spotted the tracers from a laser samopal slicing through the absolute darkness that had descended on the colony. In response, the heavy plasma machinegun spoke with a wolf's snarl.

"Plastun — we're coming to you!"

The two comrades came to Tytar. They had just finished off the tarantula's crew who'd been trying to abandon the machine. One of them had managed to run a few meters from the burning vehicle before going down from a single shot to the back of the head — Hordynia had cleanly put half a gram of plasma through him at full gallop from horseback. The other was finished right in the hatch.

Hordynia dismounted and, under cover from Kelep, checked the passenger compartment of the burning machine. The moment he cracked open the hatch, the body of the squad leader fell heavily out.

The entire head and face had been shredded by tiny fragments of shattered armor. No bullet wounds. No burns. Only sharp metal driven into the flesh with such force as if it had been hurled from the inside. The Hornet's kiss.

Kelep lowered his plasma samopal, squeezed his bot-horse firmly with his knees, and felt something cold seize in his chest. He couldn't tear his gaze from what had happened.

Hordynia's hand paused on the hatch for a moment, then slowly released it.

"Dead..." he said briefly, not activating the vox.

Tytar glanced at the tarantula's hull. The hole from the second Hornet shot was still smoking, the edges of the armor bent outward.

"Ox-trembling hell..."

The heavy roar of a plasma cannon jerked him sharply back into the fight.

"There are still two of ours. We need to extract them. The light support vehicle's left. It only has a plasma cannon." Tytar spoke coldly to the plastuns, not taking his eyes from the body that had fallen from the hatch. "The fight goes on. PULL YOURSELVES TOGETHER AND KEEP WORKING."

Kelep felt his stomach clench. He had fired the shot, and the consequences were plain. Even knowing it had been necessary, the weight of responsibility landed heavier than any gallop across the steppe. Every plastun knew this weight — it was the price of the oath.

Meanwhile, the volume of fire from the fighting vehicle of the narrow-eyed scum was building. From two of its sides, two smaller plasma cannons had joined the chorus.

"The vehicle is about two hundred meters out. Kelep — can you engage from that distance?" Tytar was working out the plan to kill the second armor.

"If we come in a little from the flank, the vehicle's profile will be larger. Then I can hit it. Head-on, I can't burn through that armor."

"Then we go down into the gully. Along it we'll come out on the flank and strike. Hordynia — load up."

Plastuns never needed an order repeated twice. With movements honed over hundreds of drills, Hordynia opened the case with the melt-grenades on Kelep's horse, pulled out a charge, and slid it into the tube. A small red light near the collimator glowed faintly, signaling that the warhead had been accepted and was armed.

The battle with the remnants of the other plastun element had gone static and was no longer shifting. None of them were coming over the vox anymore, but the characteristic green-tinted color of the plastuns' laser shots still bathed the steppe in its glow — the defiant light of men who refused to die quietly. In response, the plasma cannon from the roof of the armored rapid-firer put out three times as many shots, simply incinerating stones and boulders, burning holes through them. When plasma contacted cold stone, it burned through it and ruptured it, scattering dozens of fragments in every direction — fragments that could easily kill a man without body armor.

Tytar tried several more times to reach the plastuns on the vox, but in response the ether only crackled with static. They rode their bot-horses several hundred meters, closing on the fighting vehicle from the flank with maximum stealth. The vehicle itself had halted and was laying down barricade fire, while a section of light imperial infantry advanced on the improvised shelter of the nearly-overrun unknown plastuns.

"We're... finished. I'm the only one left." — suddenly a signal broke through on the vox.
"We're about to cook the vehicle. Hold on!"
"I'll take them with me. The plasma burned my legs — I can't feel them... where they are. The rapid-firer burned our horses too. Yokhym is dead." — the voice, strained with agony, was sliding almost into a howl. "They're already here!"

A few seconds later, in the pocket between the boulders where the plastuns had dug in, two grenade detonations rang out, illuminating the battlefield for an instant. Six black figures, advancing in a crouch to envelop the shelter, flared with light and went dark. Tytar only managed to catch how one of them, at the end of the flash, convulsed unnaturally and folded in half sideways — like a blade of grass snapped by a stick across a meadow.

"No-o-o! Give us a minute!" Tytar, unwilling to believe what had just happened, turned sharply to Kelep. "Opanas — silence that rapid-firer!"

Kelep had to dismount: the exit from the gully was too steep for the horse, and it might not keep its footing with the loaded Hornet. Together with Hordynia, they scrambled up the slope, with Dmytro shoving him from behind to keep him from sliding back. The extra fourteen kilograms of the Hornet with its warhead were making themselves felt. Hordynia himself was carrying one more additional round in his hands.

The rapid-firer continued to vomit plasma across the stones before it. Even from a distance of nearly two hundred meters, this wheeled vehicle was a beautiful target. Its large dimensions — five by twelve meters — allowed it to accommodate a squad of light infantry plus several small rapid-firers on its flanks.

Hordynia barely edged out from behind the crest of the gully, pressed his eye to the Hornet's sight, and within a second had launched. The grenade needed less than two breaths to reach its target. The fighting vehicle wasn't packed with electronics for intercepting mines or rocket grenades — it more closely resembled an armored truck. So without any hindrance, all seven kilograms of the Hornet's charge entered directly beneath the base of the rapid-firer's turret.

An enormous sheaf of sparks and molten metal, sizzling like pork fat in a skillet, scattered across dozens of meters around, illuminating the imperial infantrymen who had just finished clearing the plastuns' shelter. Caught off guard, they crouched, some beginning to fire chaotically in random directions. Two imperials clambered out of the top hatches: one — missing an arm, with a dark, still-living patch of plasma on his back that was consuming him alive. After convulsing for a few seconds right on the armor, his figure froze, as if welded to the metal.

All this chaos was amplified by a second grenade from the Hornet, which Kelep expertly planted right on top of the rear wheel, where the engine compartment was located. The sheaf of baked metal and chunks of plasma wasn't as spectacular as the first, but from the vehicle's rear end, thick black smoke billowed, and within a second the vehicle was ablaze like a matchbox.

"They still don't know where we are," — Tytar watched through his scope at the narrow-eyes who were taking up positions around the vehicle, while some helped their no-less-narrow-eyed comrades clamber out of the burning machine. "Don't engage with samopals yet — can you lob another grenade?"

"Of course!" — Hordynia was already descending to his horse for the third charge without waiting for the order.

"I can see five more imperials still moving," — the glow from the blazing fighting vehicle was now illuminating everything around. "Kelep — hit them with fragmentation mode."

"Understood." — Opanas took the Hornet's launch tube in his hands and pressed "FRAG" on the round's programmer. Now the grenade, upon exiting the tube, would automatically switch to airburst mode, detonating two to four meters from the target.

Hordynia scrambled quickly up the steep slope with two charges tucked under his arms; once he even lost his grip and nearly went flat on his back.

"Just don't blow us up here with that melt-crap." — Kelep was getting slightly nervous watching Hordynia's struggles on the stony slope, though he knew the grenades wouldn't detonate without the programmer's signal.

"Ox-trembling hell and the Holy Sich." — Tytar cursed under his breath. Then continued aloud:

"Kelep, hit the black stone where no grass grows around it. There are three Sunflowers lying there, maneuvering somewhere. If you aim at the vehicle's hull, the grenade will airburst right above them. How many more of them there are — only the devil's dogs know."

Tytar found himself troubled by the thought of why the infantrymen weren't withdrawing from their clearly disabled vehicle. Though the fire blazing behind them was unmasking everything within a radius of several dozen meters.

Without any command, another melt-grenade left the Hornet. At that same moment, Tytar's wisdom tooth flared with sharp pain, reminding him of the first battle in which the plastun forces had set foot on the surface. That time they'd all caught hell from drones that had suddenly descended on their patrol as it was finishing off a fuel column for the titan-tanks.

The tooth eased somewhat along with what he saw in the scope: the detonation of seven kilograms of high explosive with plasma dust directly above three infantrymen who were hiding behind a stone and periodically firing into the darkness. One of the hands that had been holding a slender, elegant imperial lasgun flew out from behind the stone, rotating around its axis, arced upward, and slammed against a rock. In the scope Tytar could see the muscles on the severed arm contracting, and then releasing the lasgun. A few hundred milliseconds later, two more torn bodies came flying out from behind the stone — the melt had simply passed through them like the tulle curtain on his grandmother's veranda.

"Bullse—" — Tytar couldn't finish; a strong pain clenched his teeth, and even his eardrums buzzed from some sound inside his helmet.

Hordynia and Kelep simultaneously spat out a fair amount of saliva. Kelep's teeth had always been sensitive to vibrations and...

"THAT'S ULTRASOUND. WE'RE SCREWED. BREAK CONTACT NOW!" — Tytar bolted, leaping a meter at a time downhill, flying toward his horse. "IT'S A DRONE SCAN. FULL WITHDRAWAL."

After these words, Kelep and Hordynia instantly understood what was happening. They dropped the Hornet with its charge and likewise rolled down the slope to their horses.

Imperial drones scanned the surface with ultrasound. In sensitive individuals, the pulse caused immediate dental pain. Tytar's wisdom tooth flared again — sharply — as if someone were pressing an iron nail into it from the inside.

The scanner drone itself was flying at an altitude of several hundred meters and methodically "pounding" downward with ultrasound, trying to locate its prey. Their previous encounter with such a device had resulted in the annihilation of a squad of ten plastuns. First the drone had dropped several submunitions on them, and several dozen minutes later, a ballistic missile strike had followed.

The imperials were not withdrawing from the vehicle. They held their positions, as if waiting for something from above. Ultrasound didn't discriminate between friend and foe, so everything that wasn't directly adjacent to the burning infantry vehicle was being obliterated from the sky.

The three plastuns had only minutes left before the drone would precisely calculate their position and deliver a strike.

"Mount up on the bots and fall back!" — Tytar was already on his horse and managed on the move to connect the neural-link cord to his wrist for more precise bot control.

"Maybe we should freeze? If we don't move, the scanner won't notice us. We'll just be pebbles on the surface of the earth," — Hordynia couldn't get the control cord for his bot-horse into the connector, then spat and climbed on anyway.

"Devil's dogs... The drone scanned us three times. The first pulse was weak — exactly when we hit them with the Hornet. Meaning we lit up like a straw bull from a fairy tale. The next scans were at higher amplitude — it fixed our precise position. Now it's just flooding us with waves. I'm going to start bleeding from my teeth soon."

And indeed — every few seconds Tytar felt as if someone were prying at his wisdom teeth, and a metallic taste had appeared in his mouth.

"So what's the plan? Where do we run?" — Kelep mounted his bot last: he had gone back for the Hornet's launch tube that had been dropped in the panic.

"We head west, about ten kilometers. Our horses are faster than that drone. There'll be an oak grove there — we'll take cover under the trees. Remember, we camped there two nights ago, by the river? That's where we're bolting."

Tytar squeezed with his legs, directing the horse along the gully. Behind his back, the sky was already beginning to gray with the approach of a new day.

"Ah! Almost forgot. Put on the anti-radar opanchas[^opancha] — to reduce our infrared signature. The devil knows what else that drone can scan with."

The three horses burst out of the gully and hurled themselves headlong across the green steppe. Here and there, stones jutted from the earth, between which the plastuns threaded their path. Their greenish-brown opanchas streamed above the horses, partially masking the silhouettes of the iron beasts and their riders from the eyes in the sky.

The pulses from the ultrasonic scanner were weak at first, but intensified every few hundred meters. The drone was clearly pursuing them and trying to achieve its objective — to drop a few small bombs on the haiduks' heads. During the last kilometer, Tytar was constantly spitting saliva from his mouth — the pain from the waves the drone was sending had become continuous.

"Listen, Tytar," — Kelep activated his vox so he could speak with the squad leader at full speed. "My contact in the mechanics gave me two decoys. They're small boxes, strange metallic shape — they reflect ultrasound back very well. According to him, they simulate the size of a human on the scanner. Maybe we should try? I'm already feeling the hits in my teeth — the drone's getting closer. Another half hour of this chase and we're done."

"Oh, I've heard about those boxes," — Tytar squeezed the horse with his legs. "We stop. Deploy the decoy."

All three slowed their horses and dismounted on the move. Kelep pulled two gadgets the size of a fist from his pack on the left side. Their facets were pressed inward and had several ribs that concentrically converged toward the center of the figure. Whether this thing actually worked — nobody knew yet.

They decided also to gather stones around into a small "foxhole," placing the two decoys inside. For greater realism, they sacrificed the Hornet's launch tube, laying it on top of the shelter. From high altitude, it might appear that someone was lying there with a weapon, covered by a parapet. At least, that's what the three plastuns thought.

"And now we go stirrup to stirrup. And like that, touching one another, we ride on."

Tytar and the other two mounted their bot-horses again.

"I figure we're still visible on the scanner," — he spat a large wad of saliva downward. "If these boxes work, the drone will see that two of us stayed behind, and one moved on. What's the better target to strike — one haiduk or two? I think two. The narrow-eyed carrion-operator sitting somewhere at a console and analyzing the scan results thinks the same. But if we stay tight together, on the scanner we'll be one haiduk. That'll buy us time to bolt while they're bombing the decoy."

"Tytar, you're a damned strategist. My thought was honestly just to lay on the ground and lie there without twitching. Maybe it would've passed over us," — Hordynia cheerfully voiced his idea, which was absolutely wrong. If a drone had already found you within a radius of three to four hundred meters, playing dead or pretending to be stones made no sense whatsoever.

At the high speed the three riders could sustain without scattering, they moved on westward. The sun behind them had already risen and illuminated the light clouds gathering on the horizon. *"If it rains too — that's pure fortune. Flyers and drones hate the rain,"* — Tytar thought to himself.

After ten minutes, the strikes against their teeth significantly weakened. And soon they died out altogether.

"Alright, now it's full GALLOP-GALLOP. The scanner's fallen behind us," — Tytar struck his right heel against a small sensor on the bot-horse, and it immediately went to full power. Which is to say, full gallop. Which is to say, thirty-five kilowatts.

As confirmation of his words, the echo of two detonations rolled in from behind.

"That would've been us. The boxes work. I love our electronic warfare techs!" — Kelep turned back, trying to spot the drone or some other abomination in the sky. "Whoa — there's smoke billowing back there!"

"That's because I also tossed a smoke grenade. To foul the air a bit after the explosion and make the drone circle longer, checking the strike result," — Hordynia was clearly proud that his tricks had worked too.

"Well, lads, you won't perish with men like these. We fly to the extraction point — we'll be there by noon. The main thing is not to run into more narrow-eyes!"

"For the Sich and the Meadow!"

The gallop gradually transitioned into a steady, economical pace. The horses breathed heavily, but without faltering — the automatics were reducing the load on their own, conserving the drive actuators. Behind them remained smoke, fire, and death; ahead — the endless steppe, awakening together with the sun.

The first rays slid across the waves of grass, and it seemed as if the earth remembered nothing. No explosions, no plasma, no screams. Only the wind and the steady rustle beneath the hooves of the bot-horses.

No one spoke. After battle, words always seemed superfluous. In their heads there was still ringing; their teeth ached with the dull echo of ultrasound; and before their eyes, again and again, fragments of bodies and armor flared. Each rode in his own silence, carrying with him that which cannot be shed even at full gallop.

Tytar looked back one last time. On the horizon, nothing was visible anymore — no smoke, no flashes. Only the steppe and the bright sky. Back there remained those they hadn't been able to bring back. Back there, the battle continued to burn.

"If we live — we'll remember," he said quietly, more to himself than to the others, and guided his horse slightly to the right, where the grass stood taller.

The three riders slowly dissolved among the waves of green, growing ever smaller, until they merged with the steppe entirely. As Cossacks have done for five hundred years. As they will do for five hundred more.

[^wild-field]: **Loca Deserta-17** — literally "Wild Field-17" in Latin. The name is a direct reference to the *Dyke Pole* (Wild Field) — the vast, untamed steppe region north of the Black Sea that the Zaporozhian Cossacks settled and defended from the 15th through 18th centuries. It was a lawless frontier zone between the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, the Crimean Khanate, and the Tsardom of Muscovy — much like this contested colony world.

[^narrow-eyes]: **Narrow-eyes** (*vuzkooki*) — derogatory combat slang used by Hetmanate forces for soldiers of the Empire of the Sun. Reflects the bitter racialized enmity of a war of annihilation between two civilizations.

[^otaman]: **Otaman** — a Cossack military commander. In the Zaporozhian Host, the otaman was an elected leader of a *kurin* (company/battalion) or the entire *Kish* (host). Unlike regular military ranks, the title carries the weight of election by one's fellow Cossacks. Tytar was chosen, not appointed.

[^plastun]: **Plastun** — a Cossack scout or reconnaissance infantryman. The name originates from the Black Sea Cossack *plastuny* — elite foot scouts who operated deep behind enemy lines during the Caucasian War and the Crimean War. They were renowned for their stealth, fieldcraft, and ability to lie motionless for hours (hence the name, from *plast* — "layer"). In the Hetmanate Federation, plastuns are the special reconnaissance arm, heirs to this tradition.

[^melt-carrier]: **Melt-carrier** (*meltoviz*) — a transport vehicle carrying thermoplasma "melt" charges, used as both fuel and ammunition for melt-based energy weapons and titan-tank reactors. A critical logistical asset; destroying one can stall an armored advance for hours.

[^empire-sun]: **Empire of the Sun** — one of the three great stellar powers. Its soldiers are derogatorily called "Sunflowers" by the Hets, and "narrow-eyes" in infantry slang. The Empire is characterized by genetic modification of its population, rigid hierarchy, and a war doctrine based on overwhelming firepower and zoo-machines.

[^star-sich]: **Star Sich** (*Zoryana Sich*) — the military and administrative capital of the Hetmanate Federation in space. Named in direct continuity with the *Zaporozhian Sich* — the fortified island headquarters of the Cossack Host on the Dnipro River, from which all Hetmanate martial traditions descend.

[^haiduk]: **Haiduk** — historically, an infantryman in the service of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth or the Cossack Host; later, a general term for a Cossack soldier. In the Hetmanate Federation, *haiduk* is the informal self-designation of its fighting men and women — a word heavy with centuries of frontier warfare.

[^trident]: **Bronze trident** (*tryzub*) — the national symbol of Ukraine, dating back to the seal of Volodymyr the Great of Kyivan Rus' (10th century). In the Hetmanate Federation, the trident adorns all military equipment as the emblem of the state.

[^sotnia]: **Sotnia** — literally "a hundred." The basic tactical-administrative unit of the Cossack army, equivalent to a company. The term dates to the 16th century and is preserved in the Hetmanate Federation's military structure alongside the *kurin* (battalion) and *polk* (regiment).

[^zbarazh]: **Zbarazh Spears** — the regiment's honorific name, drawn from the town of Zbarazh in western Ukraine, site of a famous 1649 siege where Cossack forces under Bohdan Khmelnytskyi defeated the Polish-Lithuanian army. The Federation names its regiments after battles and places sacred to Cossack memory.

[^flyer]: **Atmospheric flyer** (*litun*) — a combat aircraft designed for operations within a planet's atmosphere, as opposed to void-capable spacecraft. Control of the "lower sky" is essential for ground support and interdiction. The term itself echoes the Ukrainian word for "flyer" and carries the connotation of something that belongs to the air rather than the void.

[^hets]: **Hets** — informal, sometimes affectionate contraction of "Hetmanate," used as both a demonym and a shorthand for the Hetmanate Federation and its armed forces.

[^sotnyk]: **Sotnyk** — commander of a *sotnia* (company). In the historical Cossack army, the sotnyk was a mid-ranking officer. In the Federation, the rank persists with the same name — a deliberate rejection of standardized interstellar military nomenclature in favor of Cossack tradition.

[^infamy]: **Infamy** (*infamiya*) — a formal judicial declaration of dishonor, stripping a Cossack of rank, privileges, citizenship status, and family standing. In the Hetmanate legal code, it is one of the gravest punishments short of execution. An infamous person may not serve, vote, hold property, or marry into a Cossack family. The concept descends from Roman military law but was adopted into Cossack customary law centuries ago.

[^samopal]: **Samopal** — literally "self-firer" or "self-igniter." Historically, a Cossack term for a personally-owned firearm (as opposed to state-issued weapons). In the Hetmanate Federation, it refers to the standard-issue laser or plasma rifle carried by every haiduk. The name preserves the Cossack ethos of the armed free person.

[^sunflowers]: **Sunflowers** (*sonyachnyky*) — derogatory Hetmanate slang for soldiers of the Empire of the Sun, derived from the solar imagery in their imperial iconography. The nickname drips with the contempt of steppe warriors for what they see as an effete, rigidly hierarchical empire.

[^tarantula]: **Tarantula** — a multi-legged combat walker deployed by the Empire of the Sun. The zoo-machine is highly maneuverable, possesses exceptional cross-country capability, and can remain operational even after losing multiple limbs. Imperial engineers modeled their walkers on Terran arthropods for reasons of psychological warfare as much as tactical utility.

[^battle-cry]: **"For the Stars and the Sich!"** — the battle cry of the Hetmanate Federation's armed forces. It echoes the historical Cossack cry *"For the Sich and the Freedom!"* while adapting it to an interstellar age. The *Sich* remains the symbolic heart of Cossack identity even among the stars.

[^dzhigitovka]: **Dzhigitovka** — the art of trick riding and combat from horseback, developed by the Cossacks and other steppe peoples over centuries of mounted warfare. In the age of bot-horses, the same skills translate directly — the machine may be iron, but the rider's balance and nerve remain purely human.

[^oseledets]: **Oseledets** — the distinctive Cossack topknot or scalp-lock, a long lock of hair left on an otherwise shaved head. It was both a mark of Cossack identity and, in legend, a "handle" by which a fallen warrior's soul could be pulled to heaven. The sight of a gray oseledets on a dead plastun is a gut-punch to any haiduk.

[^prih-skok]: **"Prih-Skok"** (*Jump-Hop*) — a meditative training chant used by plastuns to achieve breath control and firing steadiness under extreme stress. The technique descends from Cossack marksmanship traditions and has been refined over generations into a biofeedback method that borders on self-hypnosis.

[^zoo-machines]: **Zoo-machines** — Hetmanate slang for the Empire of the Sun's biomorphic combat vehicles (spider-walkers, scorpion-tanks, etc.). The term reflects the visceral unease these machines provoke: they are not merely vehicles but predators, designed to terrify as much as to kill.

[^djura]: **Djura** — a Cossack apprentice or squire, typically a young man in training to become a full-fledged warrior. In the Hetmanate Federation, the term is still used for recruits and junior soldiers who have not yet earned their place in a combat sotnia.

[^terek]: **Terek Cossacks** — one of the historical Cossack hosts, settled along the Terek River in the northern Caucasus. They were renowned as mountain fighters and scouts. In the Hetmanate's founding narrative, all the old Cossack hosts — Zaporozhian, Don, Terek, Kuban, and others — united under the Great Hetman during the expansion of Hetmanate power on Old Earth.

[^kish]: **Kish Council** (*Koshova Rada*) — the supreme governing body of the Zaporozhian Host, and later of the Hetmanate Federation. The *kish* itself originally meant the fortified camp or headquarters of the Cossack army. The Council is the Federation's highest legislative and executive body, preserving the Cossack tradition of military democracy among the stars.

[^opancha]: **Opancha** — a traditional Cossack cloak or mantle, worn over the shoulders. In the Hetmanate Federation, the term is applied to the multispectral camouflage cloaks used by plastuns to reduce their thermal and radar signature. The old form persists, repurposed for a new kind of stealth.

[^battle-cry-end]: The final cry of *"For the Sich and the Meadow!"* (*Za Sich i Luh!*) references the *Velykyi Luh* (Great Meadow) — the vast floodplain of the Dnipro River that was the heartland of the Zaporozhian Cossacks. The "Meadow" was the Cossack motherland, a nearly impassable labyrinth of islands, channels, and forests where the Host made its home. To invoke it is to invoke the deepest root of Cossack identity — the wild, free land that birthed them.
